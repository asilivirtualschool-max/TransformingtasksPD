Designing Worthwhile Tasks with AI

Five certificated courses on the same question: what thinking does this task actually demand of a student? Not what the topic is, and not what the verb in the instruction says — what a student has to do in their head, and whether they could finish without doing much at all.

Work through them in order, at your own pace. Everything saves in your browser. Delivered live, they run as six sessions across two weeks — Monday, Wednesday and Friday — with each task taught the day after it is built, and a 30-day clinic to close.

How the programme runs

How each course works

  • Six panels — an introduction, four modules, and a portfolio workspace. PD 5 adds a live builder you can use on its own.
  • Progress and answers save to your browser. No account, no server, nothing uploaded. localStorage only.
  • Self-test questions give feedback on wrong answers, not only the right one. Open questions have a model answer you can reveal.
  • The portfolio workspace collects the three assessed pieces and exports them as a text file you can send to whoever is certifying.
  • Print or save as PDF expands every panel, so a whole course prints as a readable document.

What these courses will not do

  • No lesson observation. All four are portfolio-assessed. Nobody watches you teach.
  • Nothing goes to your school as information about your teaching.
  • No examination. Three pieces of your own work per course, on your own material.
  • No statutory accreditation. These carry no licence credit in the UAE, the wider Gulf or Kenya. We say so here rather than in the small print.
One rule on AI. No student names, no identifiable student work, and no photographed exit tickets go into any AI tool. Aggregate counts and anonymised quotations only — and check your school's AI policy before the first lesson, not after it.

Attribution and source

These courses teach the Transforming Tasks strategies — four strategies and sixteen techniques for increasing the intellectual challenge of learning tasks, and the twenty formative assessment check-ins — which were developed and published by the South Australian Department for Education within its Teaching for Effective Learning (TfEL) framework.

Forge is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or acting on behalf of the South Australian Department for Education. The framework is theirs. The examples, the module structure, the assessment design and the AI application in these courses are ours.

The Prompt Builder links out to the Department’s own published exemplar PDFs rather than reproducing them, which is the distinction that matters. The prompting architecture itself — MAP, AIM, CRUCIBLE, OCEAN — is not theirs and is used here with our own classroom adaptation.

Other work drawn on and credited in the courses: cognitive demand (Stein, Smith, Henningsen & Silver); fluency and fluency plus (Prof. Martin Westwell); Bloom's revised taxonomy, Webb's Depth of Knowledge and SOLO; mindset (Carol Dweck); Socratic questioning (Richard Paul & Linda Elder; Michael Pohl); Three Act structure (Dan Meyer); Three Act Science (Neil Atkin).

Before any commercial use. The licensing position for the underlying Transforming Tasks materials must be confirmed in writing with the South Australian Department for Education. Teaching the framework with original examples and attribution is a different thing from redistributing their handouts, and only the first is what this site does — but the distinction should be checked rather than assumed.
